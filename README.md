A short poem about permission — the power to ask and to be given.

> The Needle
>
> She threaded every needle
> when my fingers were small,
> her hands steady then.
>
> Now her eyes blur.
> Hold still, I say,
> and thread it myself —
> one thin silver line —
> and set it back in her palm,
> the stitch still hers to make.

A mother threaded every needle for her child; now the child threads it for her — and gives the needle back, because the stitch is still hers to make. The gift is not the needle: it is the hand staying open long enough for the other to take it.
